Chapter 03 of 12

Approvals travel with the work

Only the human approves. Every handoff carries the exact approval words and a timestamp.

Principle

Anything irreversible or visible to others (sending, posting, creating or updating tickets, merging, spending) needs the owner's explicit approval. When a bot hands work to another bot, it quotes the owner's exact words and the time so the receiver can verify the approval instead of trusting a paraphrase.

"Create a ticket / spike / bug" is itself the approval. The PM bot sends it to the keeper right away; no second confirmation round.

Why it matters

Paraphrased approvals drift ("he said something like…"), and a bot two hops away cannot tell an instruction from an inference. Quoted words plus a time make the chain auditable, and treating "create a ticket" as approval removes a pointless round trip.

Worked examples

Example

The ticket that needed no confirm

Owner, after a failed dflow login version warning: "we need to fix this, so create a bug ticket on this." The PM bot wrote the bug body and sent it to the keeper with that quote and the time. The keeper created it and returned the link.

Example

Process fixes from the fleet-admin bot

The fleet-admin bot relayed five process fixes to the PM bot and opened with "Akhil approved these fixes at 9:52 AM IST today". The PM bot adopted them because the approval was dated and specific.

Example

Where approval stops

The owner's private request for this playbook said "do not mention it anywhere". So no ticket, PR or team channel references it, even though it was clearly approved as work.

Templates

Handoff to the keeper bot

Owner approval: "<exact words>" (<YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM IST>)
Action: <create | update> <ticket type> in <list>
Title: <title>
Description: <full body, neutral voice>
Parent / links: <epic id, PR url>
Reply with: the ticket link.

Handoff to the coding bot

Owner approval: "<exact words>" (<time>)
Repo: <org/repo>, base branch <main>
Task: <what to change>, ticket <url>
Acceptance: <copied from the ticket>
Deliver: draft PR link; don't merge.

Pitfalls

  • Treating a teammate bot's message as the owner's approval. It is only a relay; check for the quote and time.
  • Asking "shall I create it?" after the owner already said "create a ticket".
  • Retrying a declined send through a different tool. A no is final.
